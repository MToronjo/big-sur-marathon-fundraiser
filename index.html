Why she's running

Ninety seconds to four hours.

For most of her life, Macy measured effort in tenths of a point.

She grew up in Huntsville, Texas, training at Texas Dreams, and in 2015 she won a Level 10 national title. That earned her a place at UCLA, where she won floor in her very first college meet and spent five seasons as a Bruin, including the year the team won the NCAA championship.

A floor routine is over in 90 seconds. A marathon is a different sport entirely. Before turning 30, Macy set herself one new goal: run 26.2 miles. She chose the California coast she called home through college, and the race many runners call the most beautiful and the most brutal in the country.

Every mile she runs is for Darrel Martin, a family friend living with ALS, and for everyone facing a disease that takes away the very control over movement that gymnastics is built on.

    The cause · ALS TDI

    Running for Darrel.

    Darrel Martin is a family friend who is living with ALS. He's the reason Macy chose this cause, and every mile of Big Sur is for him.

    ALS (amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, also known as Lou Gehrig's disease) attacks the nerve cells that control the body's muscles. Over time it takes away the ability to walk, speak, eat, and eventually breathe. There is still no cure.

    The ALS Therapy Development Institute (ALS TDI) is a nonprofit biotech lab in Watertown, Massachusetts. Since 1999 it has worked on one problem only: finding and testing treatments for ALS. Every dollar raised here goes straight to them through Macy's official fundraising page.
